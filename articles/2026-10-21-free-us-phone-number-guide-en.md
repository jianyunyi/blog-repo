# Three Ways to Get a Free US Phone Number in 2026: TextNow vs Google Voice vs TextFree

**One-line takeaway:** Want a free US number for calls and texts? Each of the three routes has a hard requirement — pick your route by what you need it for, follow the steps, and get your number in 20 minutes while dodging the two big traps: "verification codes never arrive" and "my number got reclaimed."

## What You Need

- A phone (iOS / Android, for the apps)
- An email address (to register TextNow / TextFree)
- For the Google Voice route, additionally: a Google account + an existing non-VoIP US phone number (for verification) + a US IP address (most datacenter VPNs fail its IP check)

## Step 1: Decide What It's For, Then Pick a Route

- **Just calls and texts, no verification codes → Route A: TextNow** (lowest barrier — email sign-up only)
- **You already have a US SIM / US number → Route B: Google Voice** (best quality: free US number, unlimited US calls and texts, voicemail transcription)
- **Want a spare number as backup → Route C: TextFree** (by Pinger, free ad-supported tier)

One universal truth first: **all free numbers are non-fixed VoIP numbers, and platform risk systems flag them constantly** — never tie a free number to important accounts like banks or payment services.

## Step 2: Route A — TextNow (Best for Beginners)

1. Search **TextNow** in your app store and install it.
2. Register with an email address (Google / Apple one-tap login also works) — **no existing phone number required**, which is why the barrier is lowest.
3. Pick an area code (e.g. 212 for New York, 415 for San Francisco), choose a number, and it's yours.
4. Tip: registering from a US IP goes more smoothly. The app has plenty of ads — tolerate them.

Honest gotchas:
- **Verification codes are unreliable:** many platforms reject TextNow numbers outright. Stable code reception requires the paid Lock In Number feature — don't count on the free tier for this.
- **Numbers get reclaimed:** a brand-new number is reclaimed after just 24 hours of inactivity; keep using it periodically afterward.
- Its number pool is heavily flagged by platforms, so failing new-account sign-ups is normal.

## Step 3: Route B — Google Voice (If You Have a US Number)

Google Voice (personal edition) is the honor student of free numbers: free US number, unlimited US calls and texts, voicemail-to-text. Note that it's designed for US residents, and it has two hard requirements — stated plainly:

1. Go to voice.google.com and sign in with your Google account.
2. Choose a number you like.
3. **Link an existing non-VoIP US phone number for verification** — a hard requirement. No US SIM or US number, no Google Voice.
4. You need a **US IP address** throughout, and most datacenter VPNs fail its IP check; a residential IP works better.
5. After setup: the number is reclaimed after long inactivity, and if your linked verification number expires, re-link a new one promptly or you may lose the GV number.

Also: WhatsApp and most similar platforms reject GV numbers (VoIP flag) — don't use it to register those.

## Step 4: Route C — TextFree (Backup Option)

TextFree by Pinger, free ad-supported tier:

1. Download the app, register with email, pick a number — the flow is nearly identical to TextNow.
2. Reclaim rule: **reclaimed after 30 days with no outgoing calls or texts** — slightly more forgiving than TextNow.
3. Same story on verification codes (unreliable) and important accounts (don't link them).

## Notes, Costs, Gotchas

- Cost: all three routes are $0 on the free tier (TextNow / TextFree are ad-supported; GV personal is free).
- Google Voice's two hard requirements (US IP + non-VoIP US number verification) can't be bypassed — if you don't have them, don't force it; just use TextNow.
- Not receiving verification codes on free numbers is the norm, not your mistake.
- Never link free VoIP numbers to important accounts (banks, payments, payouts) — if the number is reclaimed or banned, there's no appeal.
- Keeping a number alive is simple: use it once in a while — a call or a text is enough.
