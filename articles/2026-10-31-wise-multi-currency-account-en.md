# Open a Free Wise Multi-Currency Account: One Account for 40+ Currencies

**One-line summary:** The most annoying part of running a small cross-border business isn't finding customers — it's opening a separate account per currency and losing a cut on every conversion. A Wise personal account is free to open with no monthly fee, holds 40+ currencies in one place, and converts at the mid-market rate plus one clearly shown fee, quoted before you confirm. But it is not a traditional bank: no cheques, no overdraft, and your money is safeguarded rather than covered by deposit insurance — understand that line before moving large sums in.

## What you need

- An email address and a phone number that can receive verification codes (sign-up and two-factor authentication)
- Proof of identity (passport or national ID for verification; getting USD or EUR local account details may also require a selfie holding your ID, per Wise's verification page at the time)
- The right expectation: opening and holding are free, but converting, sending, withdrawing, and ordering a card can each carry a per-transaction fee — and card fees and free ATM allowances differ by country, so treat this guide's regional figures as reference only and confirm the price shown in your own account before paying

## Step by step

### Step 1: Check Wise's homepage to see what is actually free

![Wise homepage](img/2026-10-31-wise/step1.png)

Wise's homepage positions the personal account as free to sign up with no monthly fees, holding 40+ currencies and converting at the mid-market rate. Two concepts must stay separate: **holding a currency** and **having local account details**. Holding means a USD balance and a EUR balance inside your account; local account details are a set of numbers that look like a local bank account (a US routing number, a European IBAN), so someone can pay you by ordinary local transfer. Opening a personal account is free, but getting your first set of local account details normally requires adding money to your account and completing identity verification first (the official help page: add about £20 equivalent and verify your identity, with the exact amount shown on your sign-up page).

### Step 2: Read the official help page — an "account" and "account details" are not the same thing

![Wise official help page: What is a Wise account](img/2026-10-31-wise/step2.png)

The official help page lays out the structure: your Wise account is one overall account, organized by currency balances, plus optional jars to keep money for the same currency separate. Account details are not separate accounts — they are a way to receive money into that one account. One Wise account can hold USD, GBP, EUR, and other currency details at the same time. Personal-account details cover USD, GBP, EUR, AUD, NZD, CAD, HUF, RON, SGD, and more — with regional limits (USD details are unavailable in some regions, and new RON details are currently paused, per the live page).

Practical takeaway: before giving a payer your details, confirm the currency they want to send is in your details list; if it isn't, convert first instead of forcing it.

### Step 3: Register, verify your identity, add your first currency

![Wise personal vs business account guide](img/2026-10-31-wise/step3.png)

The registration flow, cross-checked against the official site and third-party walkthroughs, runs in four parts, entirely online with no branch visit:

1. **Register:** On Wise's site or app, choose Personal — not Business. Business accounts pay a one-time setup fee for full features (about $31 in the US, £50 in the UK, S$99 in Singapore); personal accounts don't. Enter your legal name, date of birth, and address, set a password, and bind your phone for two-factor authentication.
2. **Verify your identity:** Upload your ID; the system may ask for proof of address or a selfie holding your ID. Verification time varies — don't register on the same day you urgently need to receive money.
3. **Add a currency:** Click Add a currency and pick what you want to receive or pay in. The first currency you add becomes your main account currency; you can add more any time.
4. **Add money:** Follow the page to top up from your bank card or bank account (first top-up amount and supported methods vary by region, per the live page). The top-up is still your money to spend, not a fee.

### Step 4: Receive, convert, send, and optionally get a card — check each quote before confirming

![Wise personal account opening guide](img/2026-10-31-wise/step4.png)

Once running, daily use is four actions, each with its own fee logic:

- **Receive:** Share the local account details for that currency with the payer; they send by local transfer. Transfers between Wise accounts in the same currency are free. Some currencies received via SWIFT carry a fixed fee (third-party figures: about £2.16 for GBP, $6.11 for USD — confirm on your own receiving page).
- **Convert:** Mid-market rate plus a conversion fee that varies by currency pair, officially from about 0.23%–0.35%. Before confirming, the page shows the amount that will arrive, the fee, and the rate together — check the arrival amount, not just the rate.
- **Send:** A fixed fee plus a variable percentage, again quoted before you confirm. Double-check the recipient's name and account details — a typo means delays or a returned payment, so test with a small amount first.
- **Get a card (optional):** The Wise multi-currency debit card works for online spending and in 150+ countries. The card fee is a one-time regional charge (third-party figures: about $9 in the US, S$8.50 in Singapore, €7 in Ireland — confirm on your own order page). There is a monthly free ATM withdrawal allowance; beyond it, per-withdrawal fees apply, and the ATM operator may charge its own fee on top.

## Costs, risks, and pitfalls

- **Cost:** $0 to open, no monthly fee, no minimum balance. Converting, sending, withdrawing, and ordering a card are charged per transaction, quoted before you confirm. The business-account one-time setup fee (about $31 US / £50 UK) does not apply to personal accounts — don't mix the two up.
- **Track record check:** Wise is a licensed payment institution, not a traditional bank. Your funds are safeguarded by Wise and held in top-tier banks — that is not the same as a bank deposit covered by deposit insurance. Don't park all of a large balance long-term in a single payment institution. Wise also has public user reports of risk-control freezes and requests for extra documents, so keep payment notes and funding sources truthful and consistent, use a personal account for personal money only, and never lend your account to someone else (Wise requires accounts to be opened and used by their owner).
- **Pitfalls:** ① Receiving business income into a personal account, or letting someone else use your account, triggering risk controls; ② watching only the exchange rate and ignoring the arrival amount, which is where the conversion fee shows up; ③ assuming USD account details work everywhere — some regions don't offer them, check your region's list first; ④ ordering a card without checking your region's card fee and ATM allowance; ⑤ treating Wise as a full bank replacement — it has no overdraft, loans, cheques, or cash deposits, so large or long-term money still needs a traditional bank alongside it.
