# Get a Free German Shipping Address: mailboxde Package Forwarding Walkthrough (Oct 20, 2026)

> One-line takeaway: Plenty of German shops won't ship internationally. Grab a free German address in 2 minutes, have parcels sent to a German warehouse, then forward them worldwide — all online.

## What you need

- An email address (to receive your German address)
- PayPal or a bank card (to pay forwarding fees; registration itself is free)
- Heads-up: the name you register with **must exactly match** the recipient name you use when ordering from German shops. A mismatch means your parcel can't be matched and gets returned — a real pitfall covered below.

## Step by step

### Step 1: Register free on the official site

Open [mailboxde.com](https://www.mailboxde.com/) and hit **REGISTER FOR FREE** front and center. The company has been doing exactly one thing since 2011 (14 years): German address + worldwide forwarding, claiming ~200,000 shipments a year for 140,000 customers.

### Step 2: Your German address lands in your inbox in 2 minutes

Within 2 minutes of registering, you get an email with your personal German delivery address. Use it as the shipping address at any German webshop. When a parcel arrives at the warehouse, you get another email **the same day** — that's the 4-step flow shown on the homepage screenshot above.

Pricing, upfront: **receiving parcels is free** — you only pay when forwarding. Parcels up to 2 kg cost €3.85 service fee inside the EU (€5.44 outside the EU), plus actual postage (DHL/UPS etc. by weight). Want several parcels consolidated into one box? That's an extra €3 per consolidation plus box material.

## Notes, costs, pitfalls

- **Names must match**: if your registration name ≠ the recipient name on the order, the warehouse can't match the parcel and it goes back. One reviewer lost months and international shipping fees over a misspelled name. Spend 30 extra seconds at registration checking letter by letter.
- **Check the banned-goods list first**: batteries, liquids, counterfeits etc. are excluded — the site publishes the list, compare before ordering.
- **Customs outside the EU**: shipments outside the EU get a customs declaration filled by the warehouse, but any duties are on you. Factor that in for high-value items.
- **Retro website, responsive support**: the site design looks a decade old, but users report fast email support (replies within minutes in some cases). Don't judge the service by the web design.
- **Use cases**: eBay.de second-hand finds, German Amazon small appliances/baby goods, and niche European brands that don't ship to your country.
